# NBA Luxury Tax & Apron — Research Findings

**Track:** NBA money-layer research · Branch: `scout/money-tax-apron`
**Date compiled:** 2026-10-07

## The question

Who paid the most for rings? Ten seasons of NBA luxury-tax bills (2015-16 → 2024-25) set against the championships won in that window — plus the repeater-tax totals and the second-apron casualties.

**Constructs named in this memo:**
- **Tax burden** — total luxury tax a franchise paid across the 2015-16 → 2024-25 window, plus tax paid per championship won in the window.
- **Apron casualty** — a player moved, traded, or allowed to walk (or a roster-building tool surrendered) because a team was in or near the second apron.

## Headline numbers

- **~$2.7 billion** in luxury tax paid by NBA teams across the ten seasons 2015-16 → 2024-25.
- **The Warriors paid ~$699M of it** — about 26% of every luxury-tax dollar in a decade. Their single-season record: **$176.9M** (2023-24).
- **The Clippers paid $394.5M** — the second-biggest tax burden in the window — with **zero Finals appearances** to show for it.
- **Cheapest ring of the decade:** Toronto, 2018-19 — **$21.4M** in tax for one championship.
- **Most expensive ring:** Golden State — **~$233M** per ring across 3 titles (and ~$1B if you count the repeater years that bought none).
- **Repeater tax paid in the window:** GSW $531.2M, LAC $142.4M, MIL $81.9M, OKC $61.6M, LAL $60.4M, CLE $50.7M.
- **The 2025 Finals (Thunder vs. Pacers) was the first in 20 years with two non-tax teams** (per ESPN's Bobby Marks, via Front Office Sports).

Notes: the Warriors deliberately ducked under the tax in 2019-20 (finished below the line; Hoops Rumors lists only Portland, Miami, Oklahoma City, Minnesota as 2019-20 taxpayers), which bought them one non-repeater year (2020-21) before the clock caught them again in 2021-22. The 2020-21 bills were also cut ~29% by the COVID revenue adjustment (BRI), which is why the Warriors' final 2020-21 bill was $69M against a $147M pre-season projection (Eric Pincus / Bleacher Report).

## Apron casualties

The second apron ($17.5M above the tax line, phased in from 2023-24) punishes big spenders with roster-building restrictions — no mid-level exception, no salary aggregation in trades, frozen first-round picks — not just money.

- **2023-24 second-apron teams:** Warriors, Clippers, Suns, Bucks, Celtics (first apron: Heat, Nuggets, 76ers) — Boardroom.
- **Summer 2024:** the Warriors let **Klay Thompson** walk (to Dallas) and the Clippers let **Paul George** walk (to Philadelphia), both reportedly over fears of second-apron restrictions plus massive repeater bills — The Ringer.
- **Summer 2024:** Denver let **Kentavious Caldwell-Pope** leave (to Orlando) to avoid the second apron, and dumped Reggie Jackson's salary to Charlotte — Beacon Journal / The Ringer.
- **Sept 2024:** Minnesota traded **Karl-Anthony Towns** to New York, saving $9M in salary and ~$50M in tax — Beacon Journal.
- **June 2025:** Boston traded **Jrue Holiday** (to Portland) and **Kristaps Porzingis** (to Atlanta), shedding ~$250M+ in combined payroll and penalties per ESPN's Bobby Marks/Spotrac's Keith Smith, and got under the second apron — Boston Herald, The Ringer. A Celtics front-office voice called the frozen-pick and pick-dropping penalties "real."
- **2024-25 ducking season:** Phoenix, Boston, Minnesota and the Knicks were the heaviest second-apron inhabitants; the Cavs slashed a projected $164M bill to $68.7M at the 2026 deadline, and Boston, Denver, Phoenix, Dallas, Philadelphia, Orlando and Toronto all ducked under the tax — EssentiallySports / Hoops Rumors.

## Method + honest coverage

**Seasons covered:** 2015-16 through 2024-25 (complete window); 2013-14 and 2014-15 pulled for context (the Nets' $90.57M record bill of 2013-14 was the single-season record until the Warriors broke it in 2021-22).

**Sources:**

## Patterns

1. **Spending buys a seat at the table, not the trophy.** Four of the ten champions paid $0 in tax the year they won (2016-17 Warriors, 2019-20 Lakers, 2024-25 Thunder; 2020-21 Bucks ~$0.7M). The 2025 Finals was the first in 20 years with two non-tax teams.
2. **The repeater multiplier is the real weapon.** The Warriors' four repeater seasons cost $531.2M — 76% of their decade total — and their record $176.9M bill came as a repeater who missed the playoffs entirely.
3. **The second apron bites faster than the tax ever did.** Within two seasons of its arrival: Klay Thompson and Paul George walked for nothing, KAT was salary-dumped, and Boston voluntarily dismantled a championship core one summer after winning — something the old tax never forced.
4. **The decade's biggest spenders by outcome:** Golden State bought 3 rings for ~$699M; the Clippers bought zero Finals for $394.5M; Toronto bought one ring for $21.4M.

## Caveats

- Dollar figures are nominal (not inflation-adjusted); the tax line roughly doubled from $84.7M (2015-16) to $170.8M (2024-25), so later bills look bigger in raw terms.
- "Total tax paid" includes only 2015-16 → 2024-25. Brooklyn's $90.57M in 2013-14 and the Knicks' 2000s-era bills are excluded from window totals.
- Projected vs. final: bills shift with incentives, cuts, and BRI adjustments (2020-21 especially). Treat Pincus projections and Spotrac estimates as ± a few million.
- Repeater status computed via Hoops Rumors' tracker (asterisk marks); Cleveland 2017-18 and OKC 2018-19 repeater calls follow the 3-of-previous-4 rule against gathered data.
