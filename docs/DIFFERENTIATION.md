# Differentiation doctrine — staying authentically Vector Hoops

Prompted by the 82-0 viral moment (roster-draft + season-sim pick'em).
We share a sport, not a game. This doctrine keeps it that way.

## Our identity (the positive statement)

Vector Hoops is a MEASUREMENT studio: deduction and judgment games
played over an era-honest embedding space. Every number recomputes
from source; every method is stated in-app; cross-era comparison is
done via within-season z-scores, never raw-stat addition. Our verbs:
deduce, judge, order, identify, triangulate.

## The no-fly list (mechanics we do NOT build)

- No five-man roster drafting of any kind.
- No season/win-loss simulation, no "undefeated" or record-chasing
  framing, no simulated game outcomes.
- No wheel-spin / randomized constraint drafting.
- No additive "strength ratings" that sum raw stats across eras —
  the exact naive math our normalization exists to reject.
- No lineup-vs-lineup battle simulators.

## Watch zone

The What-If Lab pairs two players and reports MEASURED partnership
qualities (complementarity, coverage, redundancy, real analogs). It
must never gain a "simulate their record" button — the moment a
hypothetical outcome number appears, we've crossed into fantasy-sim
territory and out of our own lane. Reports, not simulations.

## Why this is also strategy

82-0's virality is drafting-fantasy dopamine; ours is the "how well do
I actually understand basketball profiles" itch. Different players,
different share psychology ("I cracked it in 4" vs "my team went
78-4"), zero substitution pressure — and our authenticity claim
(recomputable, method-stated, era-honest) is the one thing a
simulation game structurally cannot copy.

## Amendment (Operator-directed): measured counterfactuals

"The Pivot" explores what-if role changes via OBSERVED historical
pivot outcomes (real players who made each archetype transition, with
n and selection-effect caveats shown). This stays inside the doctrine:
sensitivity reports grounded in precedent — still no simulated
win-loss records, series outcomes, or strength-rating season sims.
