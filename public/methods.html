Every number can be recomputed from source

Nearest seasons by cosine similarity in the 64-dimensional MTNN v5 embedding. The served model widens 14 era-normalised per-100 features to 130 features in 17 towers and compresses each season to a 64-d unit vector for neighbour search. The architecture is covered in depth in the model section below.

01

The vector space — two views, one frame

Games (fast): 12,966 player-seasons, 1996–97 to 2025–26, from stats.nba.com, per 100 possessions. Eligibility is schedule-aware — about 15% of team games plus a minutes bar (6% of schedule minutes, floored at 450) — not a flat cutoff. Each season’s 14 features are z-scored within that season. Player counts site-wide are distinct player IDs — 2,426 (distinct display names: 2,422; a few names belong to two different players).

02

MTNN — the model behind every guess

17 towers → residual towers → a 64-d fingerprint, L2-normalised so cosine is similarity → decoded to archetype (8), position (5) and skills. An 18th family, injury, feeds a durability read-out head rather than an input tower: as an input it measured −0.088 test recall, because durability is not a style axis.

h = cat([x·m, m]) → 2·d_in per family
Tower 17×: 2d_in→160 LN GELU → 160→32 LN + skip ×2 → 32-d (544 total)
+ season_emb 12-d → 556 → 556→256 GELU → 64 L2
Heads: 8 arch / 5 pos / 14 next / 18 skill towers — 12 core + 6 wide (64→16→1)

03

The map

Two maps, both PCA(3), both min-max scaled. The league map (Atlas, Play) flattens the 14 era-normalised stat traits — its three axes keep about 62% of those traits’ variance. The explorer map above flattens the model’s 64-d embedding, whose first three axes keep only about 27% of its variance — so on either map, “nearby” is approximate, and the model’s true nearest seasons are computed in the full spaces, not read off the picture. Axes are named from their correlations.

04

Archetypes — eight types

k-means with K = 8, in two spaces. The labels on players, dossiers and games come from the frozen game contract — k-means on the 14-d era-z serving vectors, seeded so it reproduces; the MTNN archetype head was trained to predict those labels (top-1 0.970 against the training snapshot). The serving vectors were rebuilt in October 2026 and their k-means re-drawn, so the head and the served labels now agree more loosely — cousins, not copies; where a surface shows a model archetype instead, it uses the head’s own assignment. The Trends stream's prevalence is computed separately, from a K = 8 clustering of the 64-d MTNN v5 embedding, with the same naming rule (names from distinctive skill z-scores).

  1. Rim protection + offensive glass
  2. Three-point volume (low finishing)
  3. Playmaking + steals
  4. Offensive glass + defensive glass
  5. Three-point volume + shot volume
  6. Three-point volume + three-point accuracy
  7. Scoring volume + shot volume
  8. Rim pressure (FTs) + defensive glass
04·b

Career trails — a player, season by season

Every player with two or more seasons gets a trail: their season points joined in order and drawn over the same map projection you see. The scrubber walks rookie → twilight, and the “what changed” strip names the two style traits that moved most each season, in plain words. Dotted stretches mean a missed season (injury, retirement, lockout).

Trails show movement in style, not improvement — a trail drifting across the map means the player’s statistical shape changed, not that they got better or worse. Nearby points are similarity, not rankings.

04·c

Insights — shareable findings

The Insights page curates seven findings about money and sacrifice: the 2016 cap spike, the most expensive seats in basketball, the max-money cheat code, the biggest invisible sacrifices, Love vs Bosh, the biggest same-team role shrinks, and the hometown discount leaderboard. They are the From the ledger series: salary, tax and ticket findings assembled by hand from named public sources (Spotrac, Hoops Rumors, Basketball-Reference and the others printed on each card), each with its source, sample and window on the card face. A ledger card never borrows the model's authority.

Every card deep-links (/insights.html#<slug>), exports a 1080×1350 PNG rendered in the browser, and has a crawler page at /insights/<slug> with a pre-rendered unfurl image. The daily spotlight never repeats a card shown in the grid below it.

04·d

Player dossier — every player, from the model

Open any player in the directory and their dossier is built from the same model behind the map — no hand-written bios, nothing to go stale. Each dossier carries a minutes-weighted 14-trait style fingerprint, the player’s archetype and how it moved across their career, their era twin (with the plain-words why), how their game changed from first to last charted season, the five nearest player-seasons in style space, and a plain-words read of where they live on the map.

All dossier numbers come from the 14-d serving projection (vectors.json — the transparent era-z style space, not an MTNN output) and the derived research assets (era twins, career trails, archetype assignments, honors). Similarity is cosine in the 14-d style space; era twins are matched in the 64-d MTNN v5 embedding. “How his game changed” compares first vs last charted season only; traits that moved less than half a standard deviation are left out.

05

League drift — Procrustes

Orthogonal Procrustes on consecutive-season shared players (≥ 30). Rotation = mean principal angle of Q against the identity; residual = Frobenius norm after alignment; chained to the 1996 root frame; RᵀR = I.

The biggest rotation: the summer entering 2019–20 (2018–19 → 2019–20) at 12.49°

Where careers end up, from the same trajectories: 51% of players stay stable specialists, 20% migrate to a neighbouring role, 13% drift, 10% reinvent themselves once, and 5% bloom late. Reinventors last longest — 9.3 seasons against 7.9 for everyone else. The steepest single fall is Interior Finishing + Defensive Glass: 16.5% of minutes early in the window to 8.0% late (−8.6 points). This section is what the Trends page showed until October 2026 — its charts were these numbers, drawn from the same drift series. After 2019–20, the next-largest rotation is 2020–21 → 2021–22 at 11.35°.

06

Skills lens — 0 to 99

Twelve skills, each a fixed linear composite of era-z per-100 stats, expressed as a percentile within the season, so a 90 in 1997 equals a 90 in 2026. Badges start at 90; gold at 97. These are the 12 core skills — the ones with badges. The model trains 18 skill towers: these 12 plus 6 wide tracking-based skills (post, transition, motor, shooting gravity, rim gravity, disruption gravity), masked to seasons with tracking data.

07 · Experimental

Forecast — where a player is headed

Three-season context → next-season embedding (first 8 of 64 dimensions). Source: the served 64-d MTNN v5 embedding, 12,966 × 64. A deterministic numpy forecaster (patch length 3, cross-variate least squares, seed 7), 7,096 windows, chronological split — train ≤2021, validation 2022–23, test ≥2024; the metrics stored in the file are train-split, research-only. No quantile calibration yet. Not TimesFM-3 (non-commercial) or a validated TimesFM-2.5.

One contract everywhere: assets/timesfm_forecasts.json, 7,096 forecasts, with an honest failure message if the file is missing.

Future work: chronological, player-aware splits with no leakage; saved seeds and weights; naive-last and drift baselines; MAE / RMSE plus angular error; quantile coverage.

Research — in progress

The Frontier Program

Four bets, each with a held-out eval gate. Nothing ships without beating the baseline that matters.

01 · Mixed-curvature embeddings

Player similarity isn't one geometry. Archetype hierarchies are tree-like, era drift is flat, matchup cycles are spherical — so we're learning a product space (hyperbolic × spherical × Euclidean) with curvatures the data chooses, not ones we guess. Challenger to our v5 embeddings, gated on a δ-hyperbolicity diagnostic and twin-retrieval evals.

02 · DARKO-style game forecasting

Per-player Bayesian state estimation — exponential decay plus Kalman filtering over every box score — with who-guards-whom matchup tensors and a minute-projection engine conditioned on blowout probability. The literature's verdict is blunt: minutes move the needle more than model cleverness. Evaluated against the betting line, never a naive baseline.

03 · Live in-game updating

Bayesian second-half projections from live play-by-play: current stat plus remaining minutes at current usage, pace, and foul state, shrunk toward the pre-game prior — because a hot first half predicts a cooler second half, not a continuation. Published baselines for this are few and far between — we're building a public one.

04 · Hyperbolic representation learning

The GGBall paper's hyperbolic VQ-VAE, repurposed as an encoder for the archetype taxonomy — the one job hyperbolic geometry was born for. Pursued only if the δ diagnostic says the data wants curvature. (We checked the hype first: the paper's "18%" claim appears in neither its abstract nor its full text.)

Full provenance for everything in the Lab: the sections at the top of this page. The Props Lab paper-trading experiment has been retired — it was a model lab in a game costume, and nothing in it belonged in the game.

        Attribution and limits

        • No lineup or on/off data — the model doesn’t know who shared the floor.
        • A midseason move is a TEAM_ID change, not verified trade metadata.
        • Tracking exists only from 2013; earlier seasons are masked, not imputed.
        • Position coverage is high but not total; unlabelled rows are masked, not imputed.
        • Archetype names describe centroid stats, not scouting reports.
        • A Chimera’s fused vector never existed as a real season.
        • Motion and music: the page transitions, the Pixel Replay wipe and the site’s music (written in ABC notation and synthesized in your browser, no audio files) follow huashu-motion by alchaincyf (MIT). Sound is off until you turn it on in the top bar.
