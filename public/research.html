Greatest Playoff Runs, Two-Way

Most "greatest ever" lists count points. Ours counts both ends of the floor. Every player-season with 10+ playoff games is scored on per-100-possession scoring, true-shooting efficiency, defensive impact counted one-for-one with offense — a prevented point is a scored point — plus a quarter-share of the team's playoff net rating, with bonuses for rounds won and the title.

score = PTS₁₀₀ + 10·(TS − .55) + DBPM + ¼·(team NetRtg) + 2/round + 4·title

All 185 defensive numbers are exact Basketball-Reference playoff DBPM values, each cross-checked — never estimated, never synthesized. (One documented gap: Shaq's '98 run is excluded because his DBPM couldn't be sourced anywhere.)

A Taxonomy of Play Styles

We clustered 2,426 NBA careers by their statistical fingerprints and let the archetypes name themselves — every label comes from percentile stat signatures, never vibes. Five families, 22 archetypes, one browsable tree.

"The algorithm independently classified Draymond Green as a point guard — 93rd percentile assists, 92nd percentile steals. Nobody told it that."Clustering result, verified by 19/20 anchor checks

Rim Runners & Protectors unites Mutombo → Chandler → Dwight → Gobert across four decades. 3&D Wings is canonical: Battier, Bowen, Iguodala, Danny Green. And the misses are informative — Dwight Howard lands with superstar bigs while DeAndre Jordan sits with rim runners, because stardom tier splits the style.

The Frontier Program

Four bets, each with a held-out eval gate. Nothing ships without beating the baseline that matters.

01Mixed-curvature embeddings

Player similarity isn't one geometry. Archetype hierarchies are tree-like, era drift is flat, matchup cycles are spherical — so we're learning a product space (hyperbolic × spherical × Euclidean) with curvatures the data chooses, not ones we guess. Challenger to our v5 embeddings, gated on a δ-hyperbolicity diagnostic and twin-retrieval evals.

02DARKO-style game forecasting

Per-player Bayesian state estimation — exponential decay plus Kalman filtering over every box score — with who-guards-whom matchup tensors and a minute-projection engine conditioned on blowout probability. The literature's verdict is blunt: minutes move the needle more than model cleverness. Evaluated against the betting line, never a naive baseline.

03Live in-game updating

Bayesian second-half projections from live play-by-play: current stat plus remaining minutes at current usage, pace, and foul state, shrunk toward the pre-game prior — because a hot first half predicts a cooler second half, not a continuation. Zero published baselines exist. We're establishing the first one.

04Hyperbolic representation learning

The GGBall paper's hyperbolic VQ-VAE, repurposed as an encoder for the archetype taxonomy — the one job hyperbolic geometry was born for. Pursued only if the δ diagnostic says the data wants curvature. (We checked the hype first: the paper's "18%" claim appears in neither its abstract nor its full text.)

Principles

Methods & Honesty

  • Real data only. Every number traces to a box score, a play-by-play feed, or a published table. Nothing synthetic, ever.
  • Held-out evals. Walk-forward backtests; the test window is never touched during development. A challenger that can't win held-out doesn't ship.
  • Null results are results. Our 3,236-projection props experiment: model MAE 3.1993 vs the morning line's 3.1775 — zero edge plays. We published the failure and made it the baseline to beat.
  • The line is the baseline. For forecasting, beating a naive average means nothing; the market line is the claim that needs evidence, and no tested model has managed it yet.
